What is a deauthentication attack?

A deauth attack sends forged IEEE 802.11 deauthentication frames to disconnect clients from a WiFi network. The attacker spoofs the access point MAC address, making the client believe the AP is asking it to disconnect. This is possible because management frames in WiFi are not encrypted by default.

How does a WiFi jammer work?

A WiFi jammer floods the 2.4GHz/5GHz spectrum with noise or deauth frames. Deauth-based jammers exploit the IEEE 802.11 protocol weakness where management frames lack authentication. An ESP32 can monitor these frames in promiscuous mode and detect attack patterns by counting deauth packets per second.

How can I protect my network?

1) Use WPA3 which includes Protected Management Frames (PMF/802.11w). 2) Enable PMF on your router if WPA2 only. 3) Monitor for deauth floods using tools like this detector. 4) Use wired connections for critical devices. 5) Consider frequency hopping or 5GHz-only networks. 6) Deploy an intrusion detection system (IDS).

What is 802.11w (PMF)?

IEEE 802.11w adds protection to management frames. With PMF enabled, deauthentication and disassociation frames are authenticated, making it impossible for an attacker to forge them. WPA3 makes PMF mandatory. Upgrading to WPA3 is the best defense against deauth attacks.

How does the ESP32 detect attacks?

The ESP32 WiFi chip supports promiscuous mode, allowing it to capture all WiFi frames including management frames. The firmware filters for frame type 0xC0 (deauthentication) and 0xA0 (disassociation). When the rate exceeds a threshold (e.g., 10 pkts/sec), it triggers an alert and logs the attacker MAC address.

Is this legal?

Monitoring your own network is legal in most jurisdictions. This tool is purely defensive -- it only detects attacks, it does not send deauth frames. Sending deauth frames to disrupt others' networks is illegal in most countries. Always use security tools responsibly and ethically, only on networks you own or have permission to test.

🛡️ Deauth Attacks

Deauthentication frames are part of IEEE 802.11 management. Attackers exploit unprotected management frames to forcibly disconnect clients from access points.

💻 ESP32 Detection

The ESP32 WiFi chip supports promiscuous mode, capturing all frames on a channel. Firmware filters for type 0xC0 (deauth) and 0xA0 (disassoc) to detect attack patterns.

🔒 Defense Strategies

Enable 802.11w (PMF) or upgrade to WPA3. Use channel hopping, MAC randomization, and deploy IDS sensors. Monitor deauth rates to detect and respond to attacks.
