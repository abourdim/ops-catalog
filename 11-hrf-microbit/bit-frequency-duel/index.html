❓ Help
What is this app?

A 2.4GHz spectrum waterfall simulator. Visualize micro:bit, WiFi, and Bluetooth signals sharing the same band.

Do I need a real micro:bit?

No. This is a simulation. Use the Transmit button and sliders to experiment with the 2.4GHz band.

What do the colors mean?

Blue = weak signal, green = medium, yellow = strong, red = very strong. Each signal type also has its own color in the legend.

How do I reduce interference?

Choose a channel away from WiFi hotspots (channels 1, 6, 11) and use lower power when possible.

📡 2.4GHz Band

The 2.4GHz ISM band spans 2.400–2.4835 GHz. It is license-free worldwide and used by WiFi (802.11b/g/n), Bluetooth, Zigbee, micro:bit, and more.

📊 Waterfall Display

A waterfall (spectrogram) shows frequency on the X-axis and time on the Y-axis. Color intensity represents signal power. New data appears at the bottom, scrolling upward.

⚡ Interference

Interference occurs when multiple signals overlap in frequency and time. In the 2.4GHz band, WiFi uses wide 20MHz channels while micro:bit uses narrow 1MHz channels.

🔧 Channel Selection

The micro:bit has 84 channels (0–83), each 1MHz wide, starting at 2.400GHz. WiFi channels 1, 6, and 11 are the most common — avoid them for best results.
