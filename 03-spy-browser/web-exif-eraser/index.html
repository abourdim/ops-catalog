📚 How It Works
1️⃣ What is EXIF Data?

EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) is metadata embedded in every photo. It includes camera settings, GPS location, timestamps, device info, and more. Most people have no idea this data exists.

2️⃣ Why Is It Dangerous?

When you share a photo online, anyone can extract your GPS coordinates (where you were), your device model, exact time the photo was taken, and even your name. Stalkers, advertisers, and hackers love this data.

3️⃣ How Scrubbing Works

EXIF data lives in specific byte ranges within the image file. Scrubbing removes those byte segments while preserving the visual image data. The result is a clean image with zero metadata leakage.

4️⃣ Best Practices

Always scrub metadata before sharing photos online. Disable GPS tagging in your camera app. Use EXIF erasers regularly. Be especially careful with photos that show your home, school, or regular locations.

❓ Help
What is EXIF Eraser?

A privacy tool that reveals and removes hidden metadata from photos. Learn what data your photos leak before sharing.

Does it modify my real files?

No. Everything runs locally in simulation. No files are modified on disk. The app generates fake EXIF to demonstrate the concept.

What kind of data does EXIF contain?

GPS coordinates, camera model, timestamps, lens info, software used, orientation, copyright, and sometimes even your name.

Is my data private?

Yes. Everything runs locally in your browser. No data is sent anywhere.
