🔐 How It Works
  1. Satellites orbit Earth at known trajectories, following predictable paths defined by orbital elements (TLE data).
  2. Using your observer location and the satellite orbit, we predict when it crosses your visible sky.
  3. For each pass, we calculate the elevation (height above horizon) and azimuth (compass direction) over time.
  4. When a predicted pass begins, the alarm triggers — flash and sound alert you to look up!

❓ Help
What does this app do?

It simulates satellite pass prediction and alerting. Learn how the ISS and other satellites appear in your sky!

Is the tracking real-time?

This is a simulation for learning purposes. It uses simplified orbital models to demonstrate pass prediction concepts.

What is elevation and azimuth?

Elevation is the angle above the horizon (0° = horizon, 90° = directly overhead). Azimuth is the compass direction (0° = North, 90° = East).

Can I change my observer location?

Yes! Use the Lab section sliders to adjust your latitude and longitude, then run a new simulation.

🌍 Orbital Mechanics

Satellites orbit Earth following Kepler's laws. The ISS orbits at ~408 km altitude with a 51.6° inclination, completing one orbit every ~92 minutes at 7.66 km/s.

🔮 Pass Prediction

A pass occurs when a satellite rises above the observer's horizon. Predictions use the satellite's orbital elements (TLE) and the observer's coordinates to compute rise/set times.

📐 Elevation & Azimuth

Elevation measures the angle from the horizon (0°) to zenith (90°). Azimuth measures the compass bearing from North (0°) clockwise. Together they pinpoint any sky position.

🛰️ ISS Facts

The International Space Station is the largest human-made object in space. It orbits at ~28,000 km/h and is visible to the naked eye, appearing as a bright moving star at dawn or dusk.
