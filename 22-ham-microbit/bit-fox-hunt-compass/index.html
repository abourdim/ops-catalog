🔐 How It Works
  1. The fox (hidden transmitter) broadcasts a periodic radio beacon signal at a known frequency.
  2. The hunter uses a directional antenna or signal strength meter to measure the received signal.
  3. Move toward the direction where the signal is strongest — the signal follows the inverse square law.
  4. Triangulate from multiple readings to pinpoint the exact location of the hidden transmitter.
🧪 Lab
  1. Multiple foxes can be placed on the field simultaneously — each transmits on a different frequency.
  2. Use the body-shielding technique: rotate your body to attenuate the signal and find the direction of strongest reception.
  3. Log signal readings at different positions to build a signal map of the area.
  4. Triangulate from at least 3 measurement points to calculate the fox's precise coordinates.

❓ Help
What is a fox hunt?

A fox hunt (transmitter hunt) is a ham radio sport where you locate a hidden transmitter using signal strength and direction finding techniques.

How does the signal strength work?

Signal strength follows the inverse square law: it decreases with the square of the distance. Closer to the fox means stronger signal.

What is the arrow pointing at?

The direction arrow indicates the signal gradient — it points toward where the signal gets stronger, guiding you toward the fox.

How do I win?

Get close enough to the fox to find it. Fewer steps means a better score — like golf!

🦊 Fox Hunting

Transmitter hunting (fox hunting / ARDF) is a competitive sport where participants use radio direction finding to locate hidden transmitters in an outdoor area.

🧭 Direction Finding

Radio direction finding uses directional antennas (Yagi, loop, Adcock) to determine the bearing toward a signal source by rotating for maximum or null signal.

📉 Signal Attenuation

Signal strength decreases with distance following the inverse square law. Obstacles like buildings and terrain cause additional attenuation and multipath effects.

📐 Triangulation

Triangulation uses bearing measurements from two or more known positions to calculate the location of an unknown signal source by finding where the bearing lines intersect.
