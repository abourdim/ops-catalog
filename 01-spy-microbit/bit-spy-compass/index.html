❓ Help
What is a magnetometer?

A magnetometer is a sensor that measures magnetic fields. The micro:bit uses it to detect Earth's magnetic field and determine compass heading.

What is the difference between bearing and heading?

Heading is the direction you are currently facing (0°–360°). Bearing is the direction from your position to a target waypoint.

What is magnetic declination?

Magnetic declination is the angle between magnetic North and true North. It varies by location and must be accounted for in precise navigation.

How does the micro:bit compass work?

The micro:bit has a built-in magnetometer (LSM303AGR). It must be calibrated by tilting in a circle, then it reports heading in degrees.

1

🧲 Magnetometer

A magnetometer measures the strength and direction of magnetic fields. In navigation, it detects Earth's field to determine compass heading.

🧭 Compass Bearing

A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from North (0°) to the direction of a target. East = 90°, South = 180°, West = 270°.

📍 Waypoint Navigation

Waypoint navigation involves moving from point to point using bearings. Each waypoint has coordinates or a bearing from a reference point.

💻 micro:bit Compass

The micro:bit v2 includes an LSM303AGR magnetometer. After calibration, it provides heading data for compass applications.
