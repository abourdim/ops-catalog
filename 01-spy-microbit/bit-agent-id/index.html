🔏 Digital Signatures

A digital signature proves that a message was created by a known sender and was not altered. It uses asymmetric cryptography: the sender signs with a private key, and anyone can verify with the public key.

🔑 Public Key Cryptography

Also called asymmetric cryptography. A key pair is generated: a public key (shared freely) and a private key (kept secret). Data encrypted with one can only be decrypted with the other.

🪪 Identity Verification

Identity verification confirms that someone is who they claim to be. In digital systems, this often uses certificates, tokens, or challenge-response protocols tied to cryptographic keys.

📡 BLE Authentication

Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) can broadcast small data packets. By including a signed identity token in a BLE advertisement, nearby devices can verify the broadcaster's identity without pairing.
